01Cover — Dataverse Ingestion Feed SLA Monitoring

Good morning. This is the Q2 2026 review of our Dataverse ingestion feeds.

Three numbers up front. We delivered 99.3 percent of feeds within SLA. Every breach now has a fix in place. And we cut our root cause patterns by 40 percent.

That last one is the story of the quarter, so let me start there.

02Quarter over Quarter Progress

In Q1 we were chasing five separate failure modes. In Q2 that is down to three.

Here is what changed. Three of our database-side problems — JDBC failures, HikariPool saturation, and null pointer bugs — turned out to be the same class of issue. They now resolve as one owned category, with one owner and one fix path.

Feed Delay and API Timeout carried over unchanged.

Alongside that: every breach traced to a root cause, every one now carries a fix, and no new failure mode appeared at any point in the quarter. — pause —

If asked about the volume increase Occurrences did go from 41 to 69, across a comparable feed count. Every single one was triaged to root cause. Fewer distinct problems, more instances of them — which is the easier problem to have.
03Q2 Performance Highlights

We processed 10,434 feeds across eight pipelines. 99.3 percent landed within SLA.

63 of 63 breaches now carry a fix — 42 closed inside Q2, and 21 in the July releases.

Zero data loss. Every affected load either re-ran or self-resolved and completed.

Three fixes are worth naming. PRIN-4619 — raised one day, resolved the next. The Drift schedule change, which removed the cause behind eleven breaches. And PRIN-5573, the timestamp formatter.

04RCA Pattern Intelligence

Three categories. Application and database bugs are 48 percent. Feed quality is 44 percent. External network is 8 percent.

The headline here: 92 percent of our breaches come from internal, addressable causes. Only eight percent sit outside our direct control.

That is good news. It means most of this is ours to fix, and we have been fixing it.

05Detailed Incidents 1 of 3 — Drift and MCA

Drift, seventeen breaches. The cause is simple. Drift finishes their load by seven. We were reading at eight. When they ran late — and they did — we read before their data was there, and got nulls.

The fix was equally simple. We moved our job to 8:30. That window is now clear.

The database bugs went into the July tech release under PRIN-6166 and PRIN-5183. Resolved on the 17th of July. Nothing outstanding on Drift.

MCA, fifteen breaches. Two source-side changes, and they told us about neither one.

They changed their timestamp format, and they sent us a field that was one character too long for our column.

PRIN-5573 is the important one. Rather than patch that single format, we built a formatter that handles the variations — separators, AM/PM, fractional seconds. So the next time they change it, we do not break.

06Detailed Incidents 2 of 3 — PTS, DSMT-ISG, GRC

PTS, fifteen. Their control file counts a multi-line record as several records. We count it as one. So the numbers disagreed — 7,354 against 7,356.

That one is theirs to fix. We raised the incident, and it was corrected at source. No change was needed on our side.

DSMT-ISG, eight. They made a field nullable without telling us. Every run failed, back to back, until we changed our code to accept it.

That is PRIN-4619. Raised on the ninth, resolved on the tenth. One day, and it has not recurred since.

GRC, four. Their API is contracted to answer inside sixty seconds. Under load it did not. We retrigger by hand, and all four completed the same day.

Volunteer this — do not wait to be asked That retry is manual, not automated, and there is no permanent preventive fix on our side. Every occurrence was resolved, but recurrence is a live risk. It needs an escalation to the API owner, and that is carried into Q3.
07Detailed Incidents 3 of 3 — CRS, Policy, GDW_HRDW

Three low-volume ones, quickly.

CRS, twice. Our pull process did not have read permission on the file. That one is ours, not theirs — which is why there is no incident raised against the source. Reran the next day, and it never came back.

Policy, once. API timeout at the start of the quarter. Retriggered, completed the same day.

GDW, once. A record count mismatch that resolved itself on the next scheduled run, before we had even opened it.

Which brings us to the position on the board — all eight sources resolved. — pause —

08What Q2 Delivered

To pull it together. 40 percent fewer root cause patterns. 100 percent traced to root cause. 100 percent carrying a fix. Zero data loss events.

Three permanent fixes landed, and none of them were one-off patches — each one stops a whole class of failure.

And I want to be straight about what we are carrying into Q3: the GRC recurrence risk, a reconciliation variance I will come to, and a formal notification path so sources stop changing things without telling us.

09Standard Operating Procedure

This is how the control actually runs.

Daily. The team watches the dashboard. A breach fires an automatic alert to L2. L2 raises a ServiceNow incident to L3. L3 investigates, fixes, re-triggers the load.

Quarterly. The control owner reviews every breach report, applies permanent fixes where something repeats, exports the report and uploads it as MCA evidence.

Then attestation. The MCA tester validates the evidence independently and records sign-off. Three separate roles — nobody checks their own work.
