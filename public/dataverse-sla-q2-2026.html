SECTION III  ·  MCA CONTROL ASSESSMENT

MCA Control Assessment Q&A (1/4)

Control scope, population and operation – every answer cites the artifact supporting it

1. What control is being assessed?
Daily monitoring of Dataverse ingestion feed delivery against defined SLA cutoff times. The control detects feeds that miss their cutoff, routes them for resolution, and evidences that each miss was investigated and closed.
Evidence: SOP, slide 09 · Dataverse SLA dashboard
2. What period and population does the assessment cover?
April 1 to June 30, 2026. 10,434 feeds processed across 8 in-scope data sources — DRIFT, MCA, PTS, DSMT-ISG, GRC, CRS, POLICY and GDW_HRDW.
Evidence: Q2 2026 Dataverse export, retained in the control repository
3. How was the breach population derived?
From the complete Q2 Dataverse export, filtered to MissedCutoff and MissedCutoff – LoadCompleted dispositions, with successful runs and duplicate rows removed. That produced 69 raw items, then isolated to Failed and Not Triggered states for root cause review.
Evidence: Q2 2026 Dataverse export · filter criteria retained
4. Was the population sampled?
No. This is a full population review, not a sample. Every MissedCutoff and MissedCutoff – LoadCompleted row in the quarter was examined. The only exclusions were successful runs and duplicate rows — no item was excluded on materiality or judgement.
Evidence: Q2 2026 Dataverse export · exclusion criteria documented
5. How is an SLA breach defined, and are all breaches equal?
A feed that does not deliver to its consumer by the agreed cutoff. Two dispositions carry materially different impact: MissedCutoff (did not complete) and MissedCutoff – LoadCompleted (data completed in full, but after the cutoff). A significant share of Q2 items are the second type — delivered complete, delivered late.
Evidence: Dataverse status taxonomy · per-item dispositions, slides 05–07
6. Who operates the control, and how often?
The IP Data Ingestion Team monitors the dashboard daily. A breach triggers automatic notification to L2 Support, who raise a ServiceNow INC ticket assigned to CTI NA EX US CTI GL EX CITI DATAVERSE L3. L3 investigates, resolves and re-triggers the load.
Evidence: SOP, slide 09 · ServiceNow assignment group

SECTION III  ·  MCA CONTROL ASSESSMENT

MCA Control Assessment Q&A (2/4)

Root cause discipline, remediation quality and data integrity

7. Does every breach carry a documented root cause?
Yes. All 8 impacted sources were reviewed line by line with the IP source owner. Every item carries a root cause, a resolution and — where a ticket was warranted — a Jira or ServiceNow reference.
Evidence: slides 05–07 · source-owner review, Q2 2026
8. Do the RCA category counts reconcile to the 69-item population?
Not fully, and the variance is disclosed rather than netted. Per-source categorization accounts for 63 items (App Bug/DB 30, Feed Quality 28, API Timeout 5). The 69 figure is the raw export count prior to categorization. The 6-item variance is an open reconciliation item, tracked to closure alongside the Drift review.
Evidence: raw export vs per-source tables, slides 04–06
9. What proportion of breaches have a fix in place?
42 of 63 (67%) were closed within the period. The remaining 21 closed in Q3 — the MCA timestamp formatter (PRIN-5573) on 16-Jul-26 and the Drift DB integrity items in the July 2026 Tech Release — taking the total to 63 of 63 (100%) as at reporting. No breach remains unremediated.
Evidence: remediation coverage, slide 03 · Jira resolution dates · slides 05–07
10. Which fixes are permanent, and which are tactical?
Permanent, in period: PRIN-4619 set CNTRY_ISO1_CODE nullable in DSMT_COUNTRY — raised 09-Jun, resolved 10-Jun. Permanent, Q3: PRIN-5573 added the flexible timestamp formatter, released 16-Jul-26 under the Q3 epic. Process: the Drift IP job moved 08:00 to 08:30. Tactical: manual retriggers on GRC and Policy.
Evidence: PRIN-4619 (Q2 epic) · PRIN-5573 (Q3 epic) · schedule change record
11. Were any breaches closed without a ticket, and on what basis?
Yes — three. CRS: the fault was the IP pull process lacking file read permission, so no source INC applied. Policy: resolved same day on retrigger, no recurrence. GDW_HRDW: self-resolved on the next scheduled run before investigation began, so no action was taken. Each reason is recorded against the item.
Evidence: slides 06–07, Reference column · source-owner confirmation
12. Did any breach cause data loss or an incorrect load?
No. Every affected load was re-run or self-resolved and completed successfully. These were timing and validation failures, not data corruption. Where an oversized attribute was trimmed to fit its column (GAU_OWNER_DELEGATES), the source owner confirmed the attribute is not consumed downstream.
Evidence: per-item resolutions, slides 05–07 · INC (GAU_OWNER_DELEGATES)

SECTION III  ·  MCA CONTROL ASSESSMENT

MCA Control Assessment Q&A (3/4)

Recurring risk, control limitations, evidence chain and remediation status

13. Is the control automated end to end?
Detection and alerting are automated. Remediation is not. For timeout-class failures the team reviews the error and retriggers the load by hand — there is no automatic retry. Recorded resolution timestamps therefore reflect when the retrigger was actioned, not elapsed effort. Disclosed as a design characteristic, not represented as automated.
Evidence: source-owner review · GRC and Policy items, slides 06–07
14. What is the most significant recurring risk?
External API response time. The GRC endpoint is contracted to respond within 60 seconds and exceeded it on 4 occasions; Policy timed out once. Every occurrence was resolved on the day, but no IP-side preventive fix exists, so recurrence remains the principal risk. Escalation to the API owners is the required path.
Evidence: GRC and Policy items, slides 06–07 · 60-second API contract
15. Are source-side changes communicated in advance?
Not consistently — this is an open control gap. Both the DSMT-ISG nullable attribute change and the MCA timestamp format change were made without notice and were detected only by ingestion failure. PRIN-5573 mitigates the timestamp class specifically; reliance on informal source notification remains unaddressed.
Evidence: MCA and DSMT-ISG items, slides 05–06 · source-team INC responses
16. What evidence chain supports this assessment?
The Q2 Dataverse export (population), the per-source RCA review with the source owner, Jira records PRIN-4619 and PRIN-5573, ServiceNow records INC0021438147, INC0023796809 and INC0023197471, and source-team email confirmations for the trim actions.
Evidence: all artifacts archived in the control repository per SOP
17. What is the attestation flow, and where does it stand?
The Control Owner exports and reviews the quarterly report, uploads it as MCA evidence, and submits the attested snapshot to the MCA Tester. The Tester validates evidence against control requirements and records formal sign-off in the MCA system. Q2 2026 attestation is in progress.
Evidence: SOP quarterly and attestation tracks, slide 09
18. What remains open at the time of reporting?
No breach remains unremediated — all eight sources are resolved. The last items, Drift DB integrity, closed in the July 2026 Tech Release. Two control matters remain open: (a) the 6-item reconciliation variance at Q8, and (b) GRC recurrence risk, pending escalation to the API owner. Both are control-design matters, not unresolved breaches.
Evidence: Drift item, slide 05 · open actions carried to Q3 2026

SECTION III  ·  MCA CONTROL ASSESSMENT

MCA Control Assessment Q&A (4/4)

Control design, segregation, change control, residual risk and multi-quarter trend

19. Is there segregation between operating, reviewing and attesting the control?
Yes, across three distinct roles. Operation: the IP Data Ingestion Team monitors daily, L2 Support raises the incident, L3 investigates and resolves. Review: the Control Owner reviews all breach reports quarterly and assembles the evidence. Attestation: the MCA Tester validates independently and records sign-off. No role validates its own work.
Evidence: SOP three-track workflow, slide 09 · ServiceNow assignment group
20. Does the escalation path carry a defined response SLA?
The route is defined and evidenced — automatic alert to L2, ServiceNow INC ticket to L3, investigate, resolve, re-trigger. A response-time SLA for each step is not evidenced in the reviewed material. Elapsed time is inferable only from ticket timestamps, such as PRIN-4619's one-day turnaround. Disclosed as a design gap rather than asserted.
Evidence: SOP daily track, slide 09 · Jira and INC timestamps
21. Were downstream consumers materially impacted?
No material impact identified. CityMAP consumes CRS and Drift Application Times Management. The source owner confirmed CRS content changes only when a branch or office opens or closes, so the one-day delay carried no material effect. Items dispositioned MissedCutoff – LoadCompleted delivered complete data, late.
Evidence: source-owner review · per-item dispositions, slides 05–07
22. How is the completeness of the monitoring itself assured?
Feeds that never start surface as Not Triggered and are captured, so a silent non-delivery is visible. However, no independent reconciliation to source-system delivery logs was performed — completeness rests on the Dataverse status feed itself. The Drift Not Triggered days have since been reviewed and closed in the July 2026 Tech Release. The reconciliation limitation is disclosed as a scope note on the assessment.
Evidence: Dataverse status taxonomy · Drift resolution, slide 05
23. Were the remediating code changes released under change control?
Yes. Both ran through Jira with documented acceptance criteria, a named assignee and reporter, and a Done resolution, under Parent Capability COOPORT-193 — Level 3 production support. PRIN-4619 (Task, 2 points, Q2 epic) and PRIN-5573 (Story, Q3 epic), both component PRIN_IP, team Bulls.
Evidence: PRIN-4619 · PRIN-5573 · COOPORT-193 / COOPORT-99
24. What evidence shows the fixes worked, and what residual risk remains?
Effectiveness: DSMT-ISG has had no recurrence since PRIN-4619 released on 10-Jun; the Drift reschedule removed the pre-load read window behind 11 breaches; PRIN-5573 carries regression testing across all MCA feeds in its acceptance criteria. Residual risk concentrates in three places — source-side changes made without notice, external API response times, and the dependency on manual remediation.
Evidence: recurrence data, slides 05–07 · PRIN-5573 acceptance criteria
25. Are manual re-runs authorised and logged?
Each re-run is traceable to its ServiceNow record or, where none was raised, to the per-item reason recorded on slides 05–07. The reviewed material does not evidence a separate approval step or a re-run log distinct from the INC ticket record. Given remediation is manual, this is disclosed as a control-design observation.
Evidence: INC records · per-item resolutions, slides 05–07
26. Is the multi-quarter trend improving?
Pattern count ran 3 → 5 → 3 across Q4 2025, Q1 and Q2 2026. The Q1 rise reflects finer detection, not more failure types: Q4 reported an aggregate rate with no per-source breakdown, while Q1 introduced per-source, per-date tracking. Breach rate moved 0.72% → 0.38% → 0.66%, remaining below the Q4 2025 baseline.
Evidence: Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 comparative analysis · Q2 export
