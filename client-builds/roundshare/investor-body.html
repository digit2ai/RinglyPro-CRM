02 — The Problem & Why Now

Riders improve between lessons — with almost no tools built for them

The problem

  • Coaching is scarce and expensive. A rider sees a trainer a few hours a week; the other 95% of practice happens with no expert feedback.
  • Video is raw, not useful. Riders film rounds on their phones but get no analysis — just footage they rarely rewatch.
  • Progress is invisible. No structured way to measure position, rhythm, or form over time, or to know what to work on next.
  • Improvement is lonely. The barn community lives in group texts and Instagram, not in a tool built for how riders actually get better.

Why now

  • AI vision matured. On-device pose and motion analysis is now accurate and cheap enough for a consumer phone app.
  • Sports AI is proven. Hudl, OnForm, and Coach's Eye normalized "film it, analyze it, improve" in mainstream sports — equestrian was skipped.
  • AI-native build economics. A small team can now ship and iterate a product that used to require a large engineering org — RoundShare's structural cost edge.
  • A willing, under-served buyer. Competition riders, juniors, and parents already spend heavily on the sport and have no modern AI tool aimed at them.

How the SAFE works — in plain English

A SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity) is the standard pre-seed instrument (61% of 2025 rounds, Carta). You invest cash now; instead of setting a price today, your money converts into shares at the next priced round — but on terms that protect early risk:

  • Valuation cap ($4.0M). The maximum company value at which your money converts — so if the Series A prices higher, you still convert as if the company were worth $4M. Lower conversion price = more shares for the same dollars.
  • Discount (20%). If the capped price isn't the better deal, you still convert at 20% below the Series A price.
  • MFN (most-favored-nation). If a later pre-seed investor gets better terms, early investors automatically get them too.
  • No debt, no interest, no maturity date. Clean, founder- and investor-friendly, and fast to close — no board seat or control transfer at this stage.

Example: $250K at a $4M post-money cap converts to roughly 6.25% of the company (before later-round dilution). A $750K round converts to roughly 18.75%. Priced-round alternative available on request.

Why this range — not higher, not lower. With no users or revenue yet, a buyer has nothing to apply a growth multiple to, so the price is set by replacement-cost-plus (what it would cost them to reach this point, plus a premium for the head start) — not a revenue multiple. That premium above the build cost comes from four things: (1) a working, demonstrable product; (2) the roundshare.app brand and domain; (3) the founding team — especially the CEO's professional-equestrian expertise, the “what does great riding look like” labeling moat a buyer can't acquire elsewhere; and (4) strategic fit for the right acquirer.

Three things that swing the number.

  • Does the team transfer? With the founders (and the CEO's expertise) staying, it earns the top of the range or an acqui-hire premium. Code + brand only, team walking away, discounts toward the ~$1M floor.
  • DIGIT2AI technology is not included. Digit2AI's underlying AI engine (Background IP) is licensed to RoundShare, not owned by it, and per the Operating Agreement cannot be reverse-engineered or resold. A buyer gets the RoundShare product, brand, and Company IP plus a license to keep using the engine inside RoundShare — which caps what a pure-technology acquirer pays for “the software.”
  • All three members must agree. Carrie, Lala, and DIGIT2AI each own one-third; selling the company or material IP requires unanimous consent.
Recommendation. The single biggest, cheapest value unlock is to launch and get a few thousand engaged riders + a couple of barn/trainer partnerships before selling. That moves the story off replacement cost (~$2–3M) and onto traction ($6M–$10M+). Selling pre-launch hands the buyer that upside for the price of the parts. If you do sell now, prefer a strategic buyer in the equestrian world (show-circuit/federation org, equestrian media/brand, or a sports-video platform) over a financial buyer.
